Pathway Expert: Career Overview

Job Description & Expectations

A research engineer uses science, mathematics, and engineering to develop, test, and improve technology. They identify problems, conduct experiments, analyze results, and find solutions to technical challenges.

Educational Requirements

Bachelor Degree: Usually required in mechanical, electrical, computer, aerospace, or another relevant engineering discipline. Master’s or Ph.D: May be required for an advanced or specialized research position. Research engineers also need problem-solving, programming, data analysis, communication, and testing skills. They must continue learning as technology advances.

Viability & Field Analysis

Location Aspects: Research engineers commonly work in laboratories, offices, or field-testing laboratories. In Texas, cities such as Austin, Dallas-Fort Worth, Houston, and San Antonio offer engineering and technology opportunities.

Turnover Rate & Job Satisfaction: Turnover: There is no single turnover rate for all research engineers. Employees may leave because of better salaries, career advancement, workplace conditions, or the end of a research contract. Stress: The job can be stressful because of tight deadlines, difficult technical problems, changing requirements, and pressure to ensure that systems work correctly. Watson explained that engineers must communicate realistic timelines and thoroughly test their work. Job Satisfaction: Research engineering can be rewarding for people who enjoy solving problems, developing technology, and learning new skills. However, workload, management, salary, and opportunities for advancement can affect satisfaction.

Alternative Jobs: Test Engineer, Quality Assurance Engineer, Software Engineer, Systems Engineer, Project Manager, Technical Consultant

SUBJECT: How the PID math Equation works in engineering

1. Opening

One of the most important equations is the PID controller. Engineers use this equation to make machines adjust themselves automatically such as: a car keeping a constant speed on cruise control or a thermostat keeping a house at 70 degrees. PID stands for proportional, integral, and derivative. Each part of the equations uses data from the past, present, and future to make smooth and continuous outputs.

2. Mathematical Concept Connection

The "P" in pid uses basic algebra to see what's happening now. It subtracts the machine's current states from the goal to find the error. For example if i have a car that's cruise control is set to 50 mph and its currently going 20 mph the error is 30 mph. The equation multiples that number by a set number to tell the engine to speed up until the gap is smaller then its run again until it reaches its setpoint. Proportional isn't always enough for every system on its own. Imagine your car is driving up a hill and gets stuck at 48 mph because the hill is fighting against the engine. The "i" value uses calculus to add the total errors over time. This lets the engine see that the car has been 2 mph slow for several seconds and adds the missing speed until the car reaches 50. If the engine pushes too hard to reach 50 I might go over the setpoint. The "D" part of the equations keeps this from happening. It measures how fast the error is shrinking and will slow down the engine to keep from passing 50 mph. This creates a smooth acceleration without jerking the car.

1. Introduction

Research Engineers work to develop, test, and improve technologies, products, and systems. Although their work often focuses on solving technical problems, they must also follow laws and regulations that protect employees, the public, and sensitive information. The specific regulations depend on the industry and the type of research being conducted. For example, research engineers working with communication systems may face different requirements than those developing medical devices or manufacturing equipment. THis report will focus on several important regulations that can affect research engineers, particularly those involved in radio and communication technology. It will also explain the challenges these regulations create and possible ways engineers can address them.

2. Federal Communications Regulations

Research engineers who develop or test radio communications equipment may need to follow regulations established by the Federal Communications Commission (FCC). The FCC regulates radio frequency devices to prevent harmful interference and ensure that equipment meets technical requirements. Certain devices must receive FCC authorization before they can be marked or imported into the United States. The authorization process can involve equipment certification or Suppliers Declaration of Conformity, depending on the type of device (Federal Communications Commission & Federal Communications Commission) These regulations can create challenges for research engineers because equipment must meet specific standards before it can be approved. Testing and certification can take additional time and may require engineers to make changes to their designs. If a device fails testing, the research team may need to identify the problem, improve the equipment, and test it again. One solution is to consider regulatory requirements early in the design process rather than waiting until the project is almost finished. Engineers can also keep detailed testing records and work with compliance specialists to help prevent delays.

3. Workplace Safety Laws

Another important area of regulation is workplace safety. The Occupational Safety and Health Act of 1970 established requirements for employers to provide safe working environments. The Occupational Safety and Health Administration (OSHA) is responsible for enforcing many of these workplace safety standards. Employers must provide safe equipment, identify workplace hazards, communicate safety procedures, and train employees to perform their jobs safely (Occupational Safety and Health Administration & Occupational Safety and Health Administration). Research engineers may work in laboratories, manufacturing facilities, or outdoor testing locations. Depending on their projects, they could work around electrical equipment, machinery, or unfamiliar testing environments. These conditions can create safety risks if proper procedures are not followed. A possible solution is to provide regular safety training, inspect equipment before testing, and make sure employees understand the hazards associated with each project. Following safety procedures can protect employees while also preventing interruptions to research and testing.

4. Technology Protection and Export Regulations

Research engineers may also work with technology that is restricted from being shared with certain individuals or organizations. Two important sets of federal regulations are the International Traffic in Arms Regulations (ITAR) and the Export Administration Regulations (EAR). ITAR controls certain defense-related products, services, and technical information. The EAR, administered by the Department of Commerce's Bureau of Industry and Security, applies to certain commercial and dual-use products, software, and technology. These regulations can affect how technical information is shared or transferred outside the United States. In some circumstances, sharing controlled technology with a foreign person inside the United States can also count as an export (Bureau of Industry and Security, Bureau of Industry and Security, & Defense Federal Acquisition Regulations Supplement Procedures, Guidance, and Information). These regulations can make collaboration more complicated, especially when research involves international partners or employees who need access to technical information. Engineers must understand what information they are allowed to share and follow their employer's security procedures. A possible solution is for companies to provide export-control training and clearly identify restricted information. Engineers should also consult their company's compliance department before sharing technical information when they are unsure whether restrictions apply. It is important to understand that ITAR and EAR do not apply to every research engineering project; their requirements depend on the technology and the circumstances.

5. Professional and Client Responsibilities

In addition to government regulations, research engineers must meet professional expectations and the requirements established by their employers and clients. Engineering projects often involve contracts that explain what a client expects, including performance requirements, testing procedures, and deadlines. Engineers are responsible for conducting accurate tests, documenting their results, and communicating problems that could affect the project. During my interview with Wilson Watson, I learned about his experience working with over-the-horizon radio testing. His work involves testing communication systems and making sure they meet the requirements established by the client. He also explained the importance of notifying clients when changes could affect the project's timeline. This demonstrates how important communication and responsibility are in engineering. One challenge is that client requirements can change while a project is already underway. These changes may require additional testing, adjustments to the technology, or changes to the schedule. To address these challenges, engineers should document project requirements, communicate with clients regularly, and explain how requested changes could affect the project. Peer reviews and repeated testing can also help identify problems before the final product is delivered.

6. Conclusion

Research engineers must follow several regulations and professional responsibilities while developing and testing new technologies. Depending on their field, these may include FCC requirements, workplace safety laws, and restrictions on sharing certain technical information. Although these regulations can create challenges by increasing project costs, limiting collaboration, or delaying deadlines, they are important for protecting workers, the public, and sensitive technology. Through proper training, careful documentation, communication, and testing, research engineers can meet these requirements while continuing to develop reliable technologies.

The Wright Brothers and Research Engineering


Many of the technological advancements made throughout history have been made by individuals willing to challenge already existing ideas and explore new possibilities. An example of this happening came from Wilbur and Orville Wright, whose dedication led to the understanding of flight and one of the most significant technological advancements to date in modern history. Because of their experimentation, problem solving and dedication to challenge age-old ideas, the Wright brothers demonstrated the importance of innovation when overcoming engineering challenges. These qualities remain a critical factor in research engineering, where experimentation, critical thinking, and continuous improvement are necessary for making new technologies.

The Wright brothers showed perseverance by continuing their research of flight despite being doubted and told by others it would never work. “Nothing did, not even the self-evident reality that every time they took off in one of their contrivances, they risked being killed.” (McCullough, 2015, p.1) Although aware of this case their determination shows how overcoming things and being committed to something can lead to making big advancements. Like the Wright brothers, research engineers have to be able to continue with their job when something fails or does not make the expected results. This leads to engineers to use the gathered data to improve the idea or item and make it more effective.

Testing various amounts of designs was necessary to the Wright brothers finding success because it allowed them to figure out what methods worked and what needed to improve, this would also allow them to make adjustments and make the aircraft more efficient. “With characteristic understatement, Wilbur summarized by saying they were able to return home ‘without having our pet theories completely knocked in the head by the hard logic of experience, and our own brains dashed out in the bargain.” (McCullough, 2015, p.55) Just like the Wright brothers, research engineers rely on experiments to test different designs for data and find flaws.

The Wright brothers' development of the first airplane changed transportation by allowing for longer travel across large distances. Their advancement of aircraft showed how technological innovation can improve everyday life and help future advancements. Research engineering helps in similar achievements by making or improving ideas or things that solve real-world problems. The Wright brothers' accomplishments show how research, and innovation can help improve things that can have a long impact on society.

The Wright brothers demonstrated the importance of perseverance, and innovation in achieving technological advancements. They were dedicated to developing aircraft and showed how they were willing to learn from failures to have large breakthroughs. These qualities are what is essential to a person pursuing research engineering where you will be required to be continuously testing, problem solving, and improving. Lastly, perseverance is one of the most important qualities for a research engineer to have because for something to succeed you first have to pass through obstacles, failures, and continuing to improve.
