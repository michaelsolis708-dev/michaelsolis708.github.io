Chosen Career Pathway: Research Engineering

Date: November 2026

Pathway Expert: Career Overview

Job Description & Expectations

A research engineer uses science, mathematics, and engineering to develop, test, and improve technology. They identify problems, conduct experiments, analyze results, and find solutions to technical challenges.

Educational Requirements

Bachelor Degree: Usually required in mechanical, electrical, computer, aerospace, or another relevant engineering discipline. Master’s or Ph.D: May be required for an advanced or specialized research position. Research engineers also need problem-solving, programming, data analysis, communication, and testing skills. They must continue learning as technology advances.

Viability & Field Analysis

Location Aspects: Research engineers commonly work in laboratories, offices, or field-testing laboratories. In Texas, cities such as Austin, Dallas-Fort Worth, Houston, and San Antonio offer engineering and technology opportunities.

Turnover Rate & Job Satisfaction: Turnover: There is no single turnover rate for all research engineers. Employees may leave because of better salaries, career advancement, workplace conditions, or the end of a research contract. Stress: The job can be stressful because of tight deadlines, difficult technical problems, changing requirements, and pressure to ensure that systems work correctly. Watson explained that engineers must communicate realistic timelines and thoroughly test their work. Job Satisfaction: Research engineering can be rewarding for people who enjoy solving problems, developing technology, and learning new skills. However, workload, management, salary, and opportunities for advancement can affect satisfaction.
